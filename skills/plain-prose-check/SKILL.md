# Plain Prose Check

## Overview
Mannered prose swaps a direct statement for a figure of speech or a stylistic
move. "A parameter worth varying" becomes "a dial worth turning"; "this still
matters" becomes "this earns its keep." These moves show off the writer instead
of carrying the idea, and readers notice. They also blur meaning: a metaphor
brings associations the writer did not pick and cannot control.

Core principle: **say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use
it.**

(The standard this skill encodes is Alex Galle-From's prose law — "Melville
written by Hemingway": one thought per line, flourish earned.)

## When to Use
- Editing text someone will publish under their own name.
- Public or customer-facing copy.
- Any draft you wrote that will be read by people outside the project.
- A reader says the text sounds "AI-written", "trying too hard", or vague.

Not for fiction or verse where figurative language is the point, unless the
author asks.

## Devices to catch
| Device | Example | Plain version |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphor in place of a literal phrase | "a dial worth turning", "earns its keep", "pays the debt" | "a setting worth changing", "is still useful", "fixes the earlier problem" |
| Dash aside: a second thought spliced into a sentence with dashes | "The fix was simple, almost embarrassing, and it worked" written with dashes around the middle | Two sentences, or cut the aside |
| Not-X-but-Y: denying a claim nobody made | "This isn't a tool. It's a philosophy." | State what it is: "This is a set of rules for..." |
| Colon or question reveal | "Here's the thing: ...", "The result? ...", "One word: trust." | State the result directly |
| Scare quotes on ordinary words | the "fix", a "real" test | Remove them, or say what is wrong with the word |
| Stock phrases | quietly, at its core, a testament to, navigate, landscape, delve, lean into, unlock, in a world where, it's worth noting | Cut, or name the concrete thing |
| Rhythm triplets | "fast, flexible, and fearless" | Keep the one claim that is true and specific |
| Intensifiers and hedges | truly, deeply, incredibly, genuinely, arguably | Cut; let the fact carry weight |
| Personified abstractions | "the data tells a story", "the code wants to be simple" | Say who concluded what |
| Invented vocabulary drift | Project or chat coinages ("receipts", "lanes", "the feed") in public text | Use the ordinary word, or define it once |

## Steps
1. **Mark before fixing.** Read once and list every instance with its device
   name. Do not rewrite during this pass.
2. **For each mark, ask what the sentence literally means.** Write that. If a
   literal phrase exists, use it.
3. **Keep a figure only if it says something the literal version cannot.** Note
   why in the change list. This should be rare.
4. **Re-read the revision for new devices.** Fixes often introduce their own
   dash or colon.
5. **Do not flatten.** Plain is not bland. Specific nouns, strong verbs, short
   sentences, and the author's own vocabulary all stay.

## When the text is someone else's
- The author's voice is theirs. Change only what was asked, and only the
  mannered parts; keep their word choices and sentence shapes otherwise.
- Return a change list beside the revision: original phrase, revised phrase,
  device name. The author accepts or rejects each line.
- Lines you add (not edits of theirs) are marked as new, for their review.
- If a line is mannered but clearly a deliberate signature (a recurring phrase
  they use on purpose), flag it instead of changing it.

## Output shape
```
REVISED TEXT
<the text>

CHANGES
1. "a dial worth turning" -> "a setting worth changing" (metaphor)
2. ...
FLAGGED, NOT CHANGED
- "<phrase>": possible signature phrase; your call.
```

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Replacing one metaphor with another | Ask what it literally means |
| Rewriting the whole piece in your own voice | Edit only the marked parts |
| Removing every dash or colon mechanically | Catch the move (aside, reveal), not the character |
| Adding "clarity" with longer sentences | Shorter, more specific |
| Silent edits | Change list, every time |
