# Pitch and Pick

## Overview
The first idea that sounds good is usually picked because it came first.
Getting several independent pitches, scoring them against criteria written
before reading any of them, and keeping the best parts of the losers gives a
better choice and a record of why it was made.

Core principle: **rubric before pitches, pitches made independently, and the
runner-ups' best ideas written down to reuse.**

## When to Use
- The space of possible projects or designs is open.
- There is a fixed time budget and a wrong pick costs most of it.
- More than one agent, model, or person could propose.

Not for choices with one obvious answer, or small reversible ones.

## Steps
1. **Write the rubric first.** 3 to 6 criteria with weights, tied to the actual
   goal and constraints (time, hardware, audience, risk). Name the tiebreak
   criterion now ("if within 1 point, the one with less unproven tech wins").
2. **Get independent pitches.** Each pitcher (a separate agent, model, or a
   fresh context) gets the same goal, constraints, and rubric, and sees no other
   pitch. Ask for: one-paragraph concept, the riskiest assumption, how it would
   be proven early, and what it cuts if time runs short.
3. **Score each pitch against the rubric**, criterion by criterion, with a
   one-line reason per score. If possible, have two judges score separately and
   compare; where they disagree by more than a point, read both reasons.
4. **Pick by the scores and the tiebreak** you wrote in step 1. If you overrule
   the scores, say why in one line.
5. **List the steals.** For each losing pitch, the one or two features worth
   bringing into the winner. Decide which go in now and which are parked.
6. **Set the kill gate** for the winner's riskiest assumption: by an early
   checkpoint (about the first fifth of the budget), show it working, or switch
   to a named fallback. The fallback is often the runner-up. Write it down now.
7. **Record the decision**: rubric, scores, pick, steals, kill gate, fallback,
   in a file in the repo.

## Rubric starter (adapt, don't copy)
| Criterion | Weight | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Fit to goal | 3 | Does it deliver what the person actually wants? |
| Feasible in budget | 3 | Can the core be shown working early? |
| Distinctive | 2 | Would anyone remember it next week? |
| Risk | 2 | How much rests on something unproven? |
| Reuse | 1 | Does it build on what exists? |

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rubric written after reading pitches | Rubric and tiebreak first |
| Pitchers see each other's ideas | Independent contexts |
| Picking by gut, then scoring to match | Score first; explain any overrule |
| Losers discarded whole | Steals list |
| No plan if the core fails | Kill gate and fallback decided up front |
