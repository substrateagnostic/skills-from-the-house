I'm a lawyer who builds things — and lately, the things I build are rules for machines.

My work isn't about slowing AI down. It's about giving it rails to run on. At its core, the question is simple: who answers when an agent acts? I've spent the last two years quietly building the scaffolding that lets that question have an answer.

Here's the thing: accountability doesn't have to mean surveillance. The Lakeshore Data Fairness Act — the bill I wrote — proves that privacy and oversight can share a table. It's a testament to what careful drafting can unlock.

When I'm not drafting, I'm writing music, playing games with my kids, and leaning into the "messy middle" of figuring out what trust looks like in a world where software makes decisions. Every project has to earn its keep.
